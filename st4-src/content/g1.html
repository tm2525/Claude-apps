Congenital 1: guidelines and trials

The guideline documents and the trials and long-term studies behind congenital practice, with what was confirmed and what is from memory.

Same guideline, tetralogy of FallotRefer repaired patients for pulmonary valve replacement on RV end-systolic volume index over 80 mL/m² together with other measuresConfirmed
Same guideline, otherConsider endocarditis in any acute or subacute bioprosthetic pulmonary valve failure; updated strategy for secundum ASD with pulmonary hypertension; liver surveillance after Fontan; pulmonary vasodilators in Eisenmenger syndromeConfirmed
ESC adult congenital heart disease, 2020Pulmonary valve replacement should be considered in asymptomatic severe regurgitation or outflow obstruction with RV end-systolic volume index 80 mL/m² or more, end-diastolic 160 mL/m² or more, or tricuspid regurgitation progressing to moderate. ASD closure is graded by pulmonary vascular resistance when Qp:Qs exceeds 1.5, and is Class III at 5 Wood units or more despite targeted therapy. Catheter pulmonary valve preferred where there is no native outflow tract. In congenitally corrected transposition, replacement of the systemic tricuspid valve is Class I for a symptomatic patient with severe regurgitation and systemic RV ejection fraction over 40%. No routine screening for cerebral aneurysm in coarctationConfirmed

Reading the single-ventricle evidence

  • The shunt choice at Norwood does not determine long-term survival or ventricular function; nearly half of patients have died or been transplanted by age 12 with either shunt.
  • The RV to pulmonary artery shunt buys early stability at the cost of a ventriculotomy and more reintervention.
  • Fontan-associated liver disease, protein-losing enteropathy and arrhythmia, not the original shunt, drive late outcome.

Status

Confirmed on 17 November 2026: the 2025 ACC/AHA headlines; the ESC 2020 key points listed above; the AATS Fallot, neonatal Ebstein and anomalous coronary documents; SVR III, STRESS, NITRIC, FUEL and the two Ross studies. From memory: the programme lesion tables, the single ventricle pathway, the adult problems table, redo sternotomy points, root enlargement, and the transposition guideline. Not opened: the AATS 2025 Ebstein recommendations for older patients and the adult anomalous coronary consensus. No SCTS or EACTS congenital guideline from the last five years was located.
