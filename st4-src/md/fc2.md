## 1. Anatomy, definitions and normal values

<details class="fc"><summary>What are the boundaries and components of the aortic root?</summary><div class="fca">From the aortoventricular junction (the &quot;annulus&quot;) to the sinotubular junction (STJ):<br>- annulus<br>- three cusps<br>- sinuses of Valsalva<br>- STJ<br>About 45% of the root attaches to ventricular muscle and 55% to the fibrous skeleton (membranous septum, aortomitral curtain).<small>TSRA ch. 77; Cardiac 6 tab (textbook account)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How does the STJ relate to the annulus, and why does root dilatation cause aortic regurgitation?</summary><div class="fca">- STJ is 10 to 20% narrower than the annulus in the young; about equal in older people<br>- Dilatation starts in the sinuses and spreads to annulus and STJ<br>- Sinus dilatation pulls the commissures apart, so the cusps fail to coapt (central AR)<small>TSRA ch. 77</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Give the ESC 2024 upper normal diameters for the sinuses, STJ and ascending aorta.</summary><div class="fca">Men / women:<br>- Sinuses 40 / 34 mm (also &lt;22 mm/m²)<br>- STJ 38 / 33 mm<br>- Ascending 40 / 36 mm<br>Conflict within ESC 2024: the definitions text says suspect root dilatation at &gt;40 mm in men and &gt;36 mm in women, or ASI &gt;22 mm/m².<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Figure 5 and section 9.2.1; Aorta numbers (k2)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How does ACC/AHA 2022 define a dilated and an aneurysmal root or ascending aorta, and why not use 1.5 times expected?</summary><div class="fca">- Dilated 4.0 to 4.4 cm; aneurysm 4.5 cm or more<br>- 1.5 times expected fails proximally: a man in his 40s with an expected root of 3.5 cm would need 5.25 cm<br>- Values apply to adults within 1 to 2 SD of mean height or BSA<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ACC/AHA 2022 part 1</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How does ESC 2024 define aortic dilatation and aneurysm, and which term does it prefer?</summary><div class="fca">- Dilatation: z-score &gt;2 (diameter &gt;2 SD above predicted for age, sex and body size)<br>- Aneurysm: &gt;1.5 times predicted<br>- Prefers &quot;significant aortic dilatation&quot; with the diameter stated, because surgery is often indicated before the aneurysm definition is met<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 section 9.2.1</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Define root phenotype and ascending phenotype. Why does it matter?</summary><div class="fca">- Root phenotype: sinus diameter &gt; tubular diameter<br>- Ascending phenotype: tubular (supracoronary) &gt; sinus<br>- Extended: equal, or into the proximal arch<br>Root phenotype grows faster, carries more acute aortic events and dissects at smaller diameters, so thresholds are lower. BAV: ascending 70 to 75%, root 15 to 20%, extended 5 to 10%.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Fig 43 and Rec Table 68; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 12 text</small></div></details>

## 2. How and where to measure

<details class="fc"><summary>How should the root and ascending aorta be measured on echocardiography?</summary><div class="fca">- Leading edge to leading edge, end-diastole, perpendicular to the long axis (ESC 2024 I C; ACC/AHA 2022 IIa)<br>- Root in parasternal long axis (short axis underestimates through plane obliquity)<br>- Ascending: parasternal long axis moved up 1 to 2 interspaces<br>- Systole adds about 2 mm<br>- TTE cannot see the distal ascending aorta and proximal arch (left main bronchus); TTE surveillance of distal ascending, arch or descending aneurysm is III C<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Tables 4 and 35; Cardiac 9 tab (ACC/AHA 2022)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What is the CT/CMR measurement convention in each guideline?</summary><div class="fca">- ESC 2024: inner to inner edge, end-diastole, double-oblique not axial (I C); ECG-triggered CT for root and ascending (I C)<br>- ACC/AHA 2022: inner to inner on an ECG-synchronised scan (class I); outer to outer if the wall is abnormal<br>- EACTS/STS 2024: outer wall to outer wall<br>State which one you used. ACC/AHA reversed its 2010 advice (outer on CT), which cut the echo-CT sinus difference in a Marfan cohort from 2.7 to 0.3 mm, so old and new reports are not comparable.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; Cardiac 9 tab; ESC 2024, ACC/AHA 2022, EACTS/STS 2024</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Why must CT of the root and ascending aorta be ECG-gated?</summary><div class="fca">The root moves several millimetres each beat. An ungated scan blurs it, makes root diameters unreliable, and produces a double contour in the ascending aorta that can mimic a dissection flap. The arch and descending aorta move little and do not need gating.<small>Cardiac 9 tab; ESC 2024 section 5.4</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How do you measure the root on CT, and what is asymmetry?</summary><div class="fca">- Maximum sinus-to-sinus (cusp-to-cusp) diameter; this correlates best with echo<br>- Sinus-to-commissure reads smaller: use the same method every time<br>- Report asymmetry: &gt;5 mm difference between root diameters (IIa C, ESC 2024); common in BAV and genetic aortopathy and causes underestimation<small>Cardiac 6 tab; Cardiac 9 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Table 4</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>At which landmarks is the thoracic aorta measured, and how is ascending length defined?</summary><div class="fca">- Annulus, sinuses, STJ, mid ascending, proximal arch, mid arch, isthmus, mid descending, diaphragm<br>- Largest diameter perpendicular to the long axis at pre-specified landmarks (ESC 2024 I C)<br>- Ascending length: centreline distance from the annular plane to the innominate (brachiocephalic) origin<small>Cardiac 9 tab; Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Table 3; EACTS/STS 2024 Table 12 footnote</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What change in diameter counts as real growth?</summary><div class="fca">- Inter-study variability is 2 to 3 mm<br>- A TTE increase of 3 mm or more must be confirmed by CT or CMR against baseline (IIa C)<br>- Same modality, method and centre, compared side by side<br>- If baseline TTE and CT differ by 3 mm or more, follow with CT or CMR<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Table 4 and section 9.2.2.4</small></div></details>

## 3. Indexing to body size

<details class="fc"><summary>Define ASI, AHI and cross-sectional area to height ratio. Why is height now preferred to BSA?</summary><div class="fca">- ASI = diameter / BSA<br>- AHI = diameter / height<br>- CSA/h = area (cm²) / height (m)<br>Weight fluctuates and BSA underestimates risk in overweight patients; EACTS/STS 2024 moved indexing to height alone. Most useful at extremes of height.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ACC/AHA 2022 part 1; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 13 text; ESC 2024 section 9.2.1</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What indexed surgical thresholds does ACC/AHA 2022 give for sporadic aneurysm?</summary><div class="fca">- Height &gt;1 SD above or below mean: CSA/h 10 cm²/m or more, surgery reasonable (2a C-LD)<br>- ASI 3.08 cm/m² or more, or AHI 3.21 cm/m or more: surgery may be reasonable (2b C-LD)<br>Both with experienced surgeons in a Multidisciplinary Aortic Team.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.5.1</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Which indexed values does ESC 2024 quote?</summary><div class="fca">- ASI &gt;22 mm/m² ascending (&gt;16 mm/m² descending) = dilatation<br>- AHI &gt;32.1 mm/m: 12% yearly risk of acute aortic events<br>- CSA/h 10 cm²/m or more: reduced long-term survival<br>- Index to BSA, nomograms or z-scores, especially in small patients (IIa B)<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 section 5.4 and Rec Table 3</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Where do the 13 cm²/m and 1.69 m figures come from?</summary><div class="fca">- At 1.50 m, 50 mm equals a CSA/h of 13 cm²/m, linked to higher event risk; at 1.69 m, 13 cm²/m equals 53 mm (EACTS/STS)<br>- At average European height, 10 cm²/m is about 48 mm (men) or 46 mm (women); 13 cm²/m about 54 or 53 mm (ESC)<br>- So height under 1.69 m is a risk factor allowing surgery at 50 mm<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Table 12 footnotes; ESC 2024 section 10.2</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Why does ascending aortic length matter, and what cut-off is used?</summary><div class="fca">- Over 11 cm is a risk factor (EACTS/STS IIa B; ESC 2024)<br>- 13 cm or more: nearly 5 times the yearly event rate of under 9 cm (ESC)<br>- For predicting dissection, diameter sensitivity is about 4%, length about 28% (EACTS/STS)<br>- Older textbook index for short stature: diameter/BSA &gt;2.75 cm/m² (TSRA)<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 13; ESC 2024 section 9.2.5.1; TSRA ch. 78</small></div></details>

## 4. Natural history: dissection risk and growth

<details class="fc"><summary>How does the Law of Laplace apply to aneurysms, and what is its limit?</summary><div class="fca">Wall tension is proportional to pressure times radius, divided by wall thickness. EACTS/STS 2024: Laplace probably governs rupture (transmural) but not dissection (intimal delamination), which is why most acute dissections happen below 55 mm.<small>TSRA ch. 78; Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 13 text</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>At what diameter does the risk of dissection or rupture jump?</summary><div class="fca">- EACTS/STS 2024: hinge points at 5.25 and 5.75 cm; event risk rises from &lt;1% to 4 to 5% at 52.5 to 53 mm (tubular) and at 50 mm for the root phenotype<br>- ESC 2024: &gt;1% at 50 to 54 mm, critical point 52 to 53 mm<br>- Older basis (TSRA): risk rises dramatically at 6 cm<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Table 12 text; ESC 2024 section 9.2.5.1; TSRA ch. 77 and 78</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Why are the old diameter thresholds thought to be too high?</summary><div class="fca">- They came from measurements after dissection; the pre-dissection tubular diameter is 18 to 32% smaller (EACTS/STS) [25 to 30% in ESC 2024]<br>- Over 60% of type A dissections without Marfan or BAV arose in a non-dilated aorta (ESC)<br>- About 60% of IRAD type A dissections were under 5.5 cm (ACC/AHA)<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024; ESC 2024; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.5.1</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What did the MESA analysis (Paruchuri) show about diameter and dissection?</summary><div class="fca">Compared with 3.4 cm or less: 89-fold risk at 4.0 to 4.4 cm and about 6,000-fold at 4.5 cm or more. These are relative, not absolute, risks. (The ACC/AHA notes give 6,000 in one section and 6,300 in another.)<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ACC/AHA 2022 parts 1 and 6.5.1</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Give typical growth rates by aetiology.</summary><div class="fca">TSRA: degenerative 0.1 cm/yr; familial 0.2; Marfan up to 0.3; Loeys-Dietz up to 1.0 cm/yr.<br>ACC/AHA 2022: Marfan root 0.26 cm/yr; BAV root phenotype 0.06; BAV ascending 0.03; sporadic tricuspid as low as 0.01 cm/yr.<br>EACTS/STS 2024: 0.1 to 0.3 mm/yr (TAV), 0.2 to 0.6 mm/yr (BAV).<br>Conflict: the TSRA degenerative figure is about ten times the guideline figures.<small>TSRA ch. 78; Cardiac 6 tab; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.3; EACTS/STS 2024 Table 12 text</small></div></details>

## 5. Elective thresholds: sporadic aneurysm (tricuspid and bicuspid)

<details class="fc"><summary>What is the default threshold for elective root or ascending replacement, tricuspid or bicuspid valve?</summary><div class="fca">55 mm or more:<br>- ESC 2024 I B<br>- EACTS/STS 2024 I B<br>- ACC/AHA 2022 5.5 cm, 1 B-NR<br>Same for tricuspid and bicuspid valves.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Tables 38 and 68; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 12; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.5.1</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>When is surgery indicated for a symptomatic ascending aneurysm?</summary><div class="fca">Almost regardless of size once non-aortic causes are excluded:<br>- EACTS/STS 2024 IIa C<br>- ACC/AHA 2022 1 C-LD<br>Severe pain usually means rapid expansion or dissection (TSRA).<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 13; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.5.1; TSRA ch. 77</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Threshold for a bicuspid valve with root phenotype?</summary><div class="fca">50 mm or more, Class I B (ESC 2024, new; EACTS/STS 2024).<br>ACC/AHA 2022 counts root phenotype as a risk factor: 5.0 to 5.4 cm with a risk factor, 2a B-NR.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Table 68; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 12; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.1.3.2</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Threshold for a tricuspid valve with root phenotype at low surgical risk?</summary><div class="fca">- EACTS/STS 2024: 50 mm or more, IIa B<br>- ESC 2024: root phenotype is one risk factor allowing surgery at 50 mm in low-risk patients, IIb B<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 12; ESC 2024 Rec Table 38 and Fig 24; Aorta numbers (k2)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Threshold for the tubular ascending (ascending phenotype) aorta at low surgical risk, and what is &quot;low risk&quot;?</summary><div class="fca">Over 52 mm, tricuspid or bicuspid:<br>- ESC 2024 IIa B<br>- EACTS/STS 2024 IIa C<br>Low risk: predicted mortality under 3%, depending on comorbidity and centre experience. Rationale: event risk rises from &lt;1% to 4 to 5% at 52.5 to 53 mm.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Tables 38 and 68; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 12</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>When can a bicuspid ascending phenotype be operated at 50 mm?</summary><div class="fca">Low surgical risk plus any of (ESC 2024 IIa C; EACTS/STS 2024 IIa C):<br>- age under 50; height under 1.69 m (CSA/h &gt;13 cm²/m)<br>- ascending length 11 cm or more; growth over 3 mm/yr<br>- family history of acute aortic syndrome; coarctation<br>- resistant hypertension; concomitant non-aortic-valve surgery; desire for pregnancy (ESC) or shared decision (EACTS/STS)<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Table 68; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 12</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>When can a tricuspid ascending phenotype be operated at 50 mm?</summary><div class="fca">Low risk with a risk factor:<br>- ESC 2024 IIb B: growth 3 mm/yr or more, resistant hypertension, height under 1.69 m, root phenotype, length over 11 cm, age under 50, desire for pregnancy, coarctation<br>- EACTS/STS 2024 IIb C: age under 50, height under 1.69 m, length over 11 cm, growth over 3 mm/yr, refractory hypertension, shared decision<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Table 38; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 12</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Give the ACC/AHA 2022 thresholds for bicuspid aortopathy and its risk factors.</summary><div class="fca">- 5.5 cm or more: 1 B-NR<br>- 5.0 to 5.4 cm plus a risk factor: 2a B-NR<br>- 5.0 to 5.4 cm, no risk factor, low surgical risk: 2b B-NR<br>- CSA/h 10 cm²/m or more: 2a B-NR<br>Risk factors (Table 14): family history of dissection, growth 0.3 cm/yr or more, coarctation, root phenotype.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.1.3.2</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What thresholds does the TSRA ascending aneurysm chapter quote, and why treat them with care?</summary><div class="fca">From the 2010 American guideline:<br>- Sporadic 5.5 cm (I/B); growth &gt;0.5 cm/yr (IIa/B)<br>- BAV 5.5 cm, 5.0 cm with risk factors (IIa/B), 5.0 cm low risk with experienced team (IIb/C)<br>- Marfan or other CTD 5.0 cm, 4.5 cm with high-risk features<br>- Loeys-Dietz 4.2 cm (ch. 78) or 4.0 cm (ch. 77)<br>- Concomitant surgery 4.5 cm (IIa/C)<br>They predate ACC/AHA 2022, ESC 2024 and EACTS/STS 2024.<small>TSRA ch. 77 and 78; Cardiac 6 tab (textbook account)</small></div></details>

## 6. Lower thresholds at an expert centre

<details class="fc"><summary>How does a Multidisciplinary Aortic Team change the threshold in ACC/AHA 2022?</summary><div class="fca">- Sporadic root or ascending: 5.0 cm or more is reasonable with experienced surgeons in a Multidisciplinary Aortic Team (2a B-NR), lowered from 5.5 cm<br>- Referral of complex disease to a high-volume centre (at least 30 to 40 aortic procedures a year) is reasonable (2a C-LD)<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ACC/AHA 2022 sections 4 and 6.5.1</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What operative mortality justifies earlier surgery, and what is the volume evidence?</summary><div class="fca">- Isolated proximal aortic surgery mortality fell from about 3% to under 1.5% (0.25% in high-volume centres) (EACTS/STS)<br>- Experienced centres report under 1% (ESC 2024)<br>- STS: 2.2% elective vs 17.2% emergency (ACC/AHA)<br>- Elective root mortality rises below 30 to 40 cases a year (ACC/AHA; EACTS 2026 repair consensus)<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024; ESC 2024; ACC/AHA 2022; Cardiac 4 tab</small></div></details>

## 7. Growth-rate triggers

<details class="fc"><summary>What growth rate is a Class I indication below 5.5 cm in ACC/AHA 2022?</summary><div class="fca">On tomographic imaging: 0.3 cm/yr or more in 2 consecutive years, or 0.5 cm or more in 1 year (1 C-LD). For heritable disease or BAV, 0.3 cm in one year counts as rapid growth.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.5.1; Cardiac 9 tab</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How do the European guidelines use growth rate?</summary><div class="fca">- Growth over 3 mm/yr is a risk factor allowing surgery at 50 mm in low-risk patients (BAV IIa C; TAV IIb, ESC 2024 IIb B)<br>- Growth 3 mm/yr or more triggers 6-monthly imaging (ESC Fig 24)<br>- Heritable disease high-risk feature: over 0.3 cm/yr<br>- TSRA (older): replace if growth exceeds 0.5 cm/yr<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Table 38 and Fig 24; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Tables 12 and 16; TSRA ch. 77 and 78</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Pitfall: why be cautious about operating on growth alone?</summary><div class="fca">- True rapid growth is rare: exclude measurement error first (EACTS/STS)<br>- Inter-study variability is 2 to 3 mm; growth of 1 to 2 mm/yr is hard to document (ACC/AHA)<br>- Measure on gated CT or MRI with centreline technique, same modality and convention, side by side<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Table 12 text; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.5.1; ESC 2024</small></div></details>

## 8. Heritable thoracic aortic disease, Turner and vEDS

<details class="fc"><summary>Give the Marfan root thresholds with classes.</summary><div class="fca">- 50 mm or more: ESC 2024 I B; EACTS/STS 2024 I B; ACC/AHA 2022 1 B-NR<br>- 45 mm with high-risk features: EACTS/STS IIa B; ESC IIa C; ACC/AHA 2a B-NR<br>- 45 mm, no high-risk features, valve-sparing likely, very low risk, experienced team: EACTS/STS IIb C (ACC/AHA 2b C-LD)<br>- Root CSA/h 10 cm²/m or more: ACC/AHA 2a C-LD<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 16; ESC 2024 Rec Table 62; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.1.2.3</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What are the Marfan high-risk features?</summary><div class="fca">EACTS/STS 2024 (Table 16 footnote) and ACC/AHA 2022 (Table 10):<br>- family history of dissection<br>- growth over 0.3 cm/yr<br>- diffuse root and ascending dilatation<br>- marked vertebral artery tortuosity<br>ESC 2024: family history of dissection under 50 mm, resistant hypertension (3 or more drugs), growth 3 mm/yr or more in adults.<small>Cardiac 6 tab (checked against EACTS/STS p. 37); ACC/AHA 2022 Table 10; ESC 2024 Rec Table 62</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>In Marfan, what do you replace and by which route?</summary><div class="fca">- Replace the root and the whole ascending aorta (ACC/AHA); hemiarch at elective root surgery lacks supporting data<br>- Valve-sparing root replacement when valve anatomy allows and the surgeon has expertise (ESC 2024 I B)<br>- Open surgery preferred over endovascular; avoid TEVAR landing in native HTAD aorta<br>- Arch, descending or abdominal aneurysm: 50 mm (IIa)<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ACC/AHA 2022; ESC 2024 Rec Table 62; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 16</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Give the Loeys-Dietz root thresholds by gene.</summary><div class="fca">- TGFBR1 or TGFBR2: 45 mm (EACTS/STS I C; ACC/AHA 1 C-LD; ESC root &gt;45 mm IIa C)<br>- With high-risk features 40 mm: TGFBR2 IIa, TGFBR1 IIb<br>- SMAD3: 45 mm IIa C<br>- SMAD2 or TGFB2: 45 mm IIb C<br>- TGFB3: 50 mm IIb C<br>Intact arch, descending or abdominal aorta 45 mm in TGFBR1/2 or SMAD3: IIb. Older TSRA figure: 4.0 to 4.2 cm.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 16; ACC/AHA 2022 Table 11; ESC 2024 Fig 42; TSRA ch. 77 and 78</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What are the Loeys-Dietz high-risk features, and why are thresholds lower?</summary><div class="fca">- Specific variants (R528H/C in TGFBR2)<br>- Women with TGFBR2 and small body size<br>- Severe extra-aortic features (craniosynostosis, cleft palate, hypertelorism, bifid uvula, marked tortuosity, widened scars, translucent skin)<br>- Family history of dissection, especially young or under 4.5 cm<br>- Growth over 0.3 cm/yr<br>LDS dissects younger and at smaller diameters; growth up to 1.0 cm/yr (TSRA).<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Table 16 footnote; ACC/AHA 2022; TSRA ch. 78</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Give the thresholds for non-syndromic HTAD genes.</summary><div class="fca">EACTS/STS 2024 (ACC/AHA 2022 the same in cm):<br>- ACTA2: 45 mm IIa C; 42 mm with high-risk features IIb C<br>- PRKG1: 42 mm IIb C; 40 mm with high-risk features IIb C<br>- MYLK or MYH11: 45 mm IIb C<br>High-risk features: family history of type A dissection with no or minimal dilatation; growth over 0.3 cm/yr.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 17; ACC/AHA 2022 Table 13; ESC 2024 Rec Table 67</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Familial thoracic aortic aneurysm with no gene identified: when to operate (ACC/AHA 2022)?</summary><div class="fca">- Time it by the diameters at which relatives dissected (1 C-LD)<br>- No such information and no high-risk features: 5.0 cm (1 C-LD)<br>- 4.5 cm with high-risk features or at other cardiac surgery: 2a C-LD<br>High-risk features: family dissection under 5.0 cm, family sudden death under 50, rapid growth. Familial disease is mostly ascending (80%) and grows about 0.2 cm/yr (TSRA).<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ACC/AHA 2022 Table 9; TSRA ch. 78</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How is vascular Ehlers-Danlos syndrome managed?</summary><div class="fca">- COL3A1 (type III procollagen); average life expectancy 51<br>- No diameter thresholds: decide case by case; rapid growth or dissection are the indications<br>- Can dissect or rupture without dilatation<br>- Regular aortic and peripheral imaging (I C); celiprolol (IIa B) (ESC 2024)<br>- Avoid invasive procedures where possible<br>- Pregnancy: ESC 2025 pregnancy guideline upgraded its recommendation from III C (2018) to I C (121 pregnancies in 43 women, no deaths, dissections or uterine ruptures; van den Bersselaar BJOG 2025); exact wording not in the commentary<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Table 59; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.1.2; Arbustini et al. Eur Heart J Suppl 2026 (commentary on ESC 2025 pregnancy guideline)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Give the Turner syndrome surgical thresholds.</summary><div class="fca">Age 15 or over:<br>- ESC 2024 and EACTS/STS 2024: ASI &gt;23 mm/m², AHI &gt;23 mm/m or z &gt;3.5 with risk factors or planned pregnancy, IIa C; ASI &gt;25 mm/m², AHI &gt;25 mm/m or z &gt;4 without, IIb C<br>- ACC/AHA 2022: ASI 2.5 cm/m² or more with risk factors 2a C-LD; without, 2b C-EO<br>Risk factors: BAV, coarctation, uncontrolled hypertension, elongated transverse arch (ESC).<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Table 58; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.1.2.5</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Why is the aorta indexed in Turner syndrome?</summary><div class="fca">Short stature means type A dissection occurs at small absolute diameters.<br>- BAV 15 to 30%, coarctation 7 to 18%, ascending dilatation 33% (ACC/AHA)<br>- Dissection 85% type A<br>- ASI &gt;2.0 cm/m² abnormal; 2.5 cm/m² or more raised risk<br>- Under 15: Turner-specific z-score<br>- Short and obese: absolute &gt;4.0 cm may predict better than ASI<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.1.2.5; ESC 2024 section 10.1.2</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Who should have genetic testing and family screening?</summary><div class="fca">- Thoracic aortic disease under 60, family history, aneurysms elsewhere, or syndromic features (EACTS/STS I B)<br>- Relatives: Sanger sequencing of the variant only (I C)<br>- No variant but HTAD risk factors: image relatives from age 25 or 10 years below the youngest case, every 5 years to 60 (ESC IIa C)<br>- &quot;Sporadic&quot; can mislead: 1 in 3 first-degree relatives of variant-negative patients also have aortic disease<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 16; ESC 2024 Rec Table 56</small></div></details>

## 9. Concomitant replacement at other cardiac surgery

<details class="fc"><summary>Above what diameter do you replace the root or ascending aorta during aortic valve surgery?</summary><div class="fca">45 mm or more, low surgical risk:<br>- ESC/EACTS 2025 valve guideline IIa C<br>- ESC 2024: tricuspid valve IIa B (otherwise 50 mm); bicuspid IIa C<br>- EACTS/STS 2024 IIa C<br>- ACC/AHA 2022: 4.5 cm, 2a B-NR (tricuspid and bicuspid)<br>- TSRA: widespread practice above 4.5 cm<small>Cardiac 4 tab (ESC/EACTS 2025); Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Tables 38 and 68; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 12; ACC/AHA 2022; TSRA ch. 77</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Threshold for concomitant aortic replacement at non-aortic-valve surgery (CABG or mitral)?</summary><div class="fca">- ESC 2024: 50 mm or more, IIa C<br>- EACTS/STS 2024: tricuspid valve 50 mm, IIa C (BAV ascending phenotype 50 mm, IIa C)<br>- ACC/AHA 2022: 5.0 cm, 2b C-LD<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Table 38; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 12; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.5.1</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>When replacing the ascending aorta, when do you also replace the root?</summary><div class="fca">- Root 45 mm or more: IIa C (EACTS/STS 2024)<br>- Lower than 45 mm in young patients, family history of type A dissection or known HTAD: IIb C<br>- A root of 45 mm or less in an ascending phenotype should otherwise be left: unreplaced roots grow 0.4 to 0.8 mm/yr and rarely need reoperation<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Tables 12 and 22</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>When do you add a hemiarch to ascending replacement?</summary><div class="fca">- EACTS/STS 2024: proximal arch over 45 mm, IIb B<br>- ESC 2024: over 50 mm IIa C; over 45 mm in experienced centres IIb C<br>- ACC/AHA 2022: disease extending into the proximal arch, 2a C-LD<br>Hemiarch adds risk when the arch is normal, except in aortopathy where a normal arch will predictably enlarge.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 23; ESC 2024 Rec Table 39; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.5.2</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Why is the threshold lower when the chest is already open, and what favours adding aortic replacement?</summary><div class="fca">- Cardiac surgery is itself a risk factor for later dissection; adding ascending repair to AVR does not raise mortality in experienced centres (ACC/AHA)<br>- Favour it with long life expectancy, root phenotype, predominant AR, BAV<br>- 34% had aortic events after AVR for AR with a 4.0 to 5.0 cm root<br>- Older higher-risk AS patients with moderate dilatation may not need it<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ACC/AHA 2022 sections 6.1.3 and 6.5.1</small></div></details>

## 10. Choice of operation: root versus ascending, valve-sparing versus Bentall

<details class="fc"><summary>Match the lesion to the operation (ESC 2024 Figure 25).</summary><div class="fca">- STJ dilatation only: supracommissural tube graft<br>- STJ and root dilatation: valve-sparing root replacement<br>- Annular dilatation: annuloplasty<br>- Aortic valve disease: Bentall<br>EACTS/STS 2024: supracommissural ascending replacement when root and arch are not dilated, I B.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Fig 25; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 23</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>When is a Bentall indicated and when valve-sparing root replacement? Give classes.</summary><div class="fca">- Structurally diseased valve: modified Bentall, I B (EACTS/STS)<br>- Non-diseased tricuspid valve: VSRR IIa B (EACTS/STS); I B in experienced centres (ESC 2024); young patients I B (ESC/EACTS 2025)<br>- Non-diseased bicuspid valve: VSRR IIb B (EACTS/STS)<br>- ACC/AHA 2022: valve unsuitable, valved conduit 1 B-NR; suitable, VSRR 2a B-NR<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 22; ESC 2024 Rec Table 38; Cardiac 4 tab (ESC/EACTS 2025); ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.5.1.1</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Compare Bentall and valve-sparing root replacement outcomes.</summary><div class="fca">- Elective Bentall: in-hospital mortality 1 to 2%; 10-year survival 78 to 93%<br>- VSRR by experts: 0.3 to 1.5%; fewer valve-related complications<br>- ESC/EACTS 2025: VSRR better long-term mortality and morbidity, similar reoperation<br>- Marfan VSRR: 7% at least moderate AR at 1 year (ACC/AHA)<br>- Not if gross degeneration, calcification or large fenestrations<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Table 22 text; Cardiac 4 tab; ACC/AHA 2022</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Reimplantation or remodelling?</summary><div class="fca">- TSRA: reimplantation (David) is most used, with fewer reinterventions; remodelling (Yacoub) leaves the annulus unsupported<br>- EACTS 2026 consensus: equivalent if remodelling has a circumferential annuloplasty; prefer reimplantation for annulus over 25 to 28 mm, Marfan, dissection, weak tissue<br>- HTAD: avoid remodelling without annuloplasty (EACTS/STS)<br>- Meta-analysis: reintervention 4% vs 7% (P = 0.07); AR grade 2+ 5% vs 8%<small>TSRA ch. 77; Cardiac 4 tab (EACTS 2026 repair consensus); EACTS/STS 2024 Table 22 text</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How is the graft sized in a David reimplantation?</summary><div class="fca">TSRA: Hegar dilator across the outflow tract, add 5 mm (wall) and 6 mm (graft billowing); usually 32 to 38 mm. Leave an 8 mm rim of aorta; tie the three subcommissural sutures first with the dilator across the annulus.<br>Conflict: EACTS 2026 consensus gives 30 mm as the commonest reimplantation graft (28 mm for remodelling).<small>TSRA ch. 77; Cardiac 4 tab (EACTS 2026 repair consensus)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What antithrombotic therapy follows root surgery?</summary><div class="fca">- Valve-sparing: aspirin 75 to 100 mg for 3 months if no other OAC indication (IIa C)<br>- Mechanical Bentall: lifelong VKA (I B)<br>- Biological Bentall: aspirin 75 to 100 mg or VKA for 3 months<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Table 38 and section 9.2.5.1</small></div></details>

## 11. Surveillance

<details class="fc"><summary>How is an unoperated sporadic root or ascending aneurysm followed?</summary><div class="fca">- TTE at diagnosis; confirm baseline by CT or CMR (I C)<br>- Re-image at 6 to 12 months, then yearly if stable<br>- Every 6 months if growth 3 mm/yr or more, or near threshold<br>- Longer intervals if stable for years, especially non-genetic under 45 mm<br>- TSRA: short interval within 6 months, then yearly<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 section 9.2.2.4 and Rec Table 35; TSRA ch. 78</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Give the ESC 2024 Figure 24 intervals by diameter.</summary><div class="fca">- 40 to 44 mm (36 to 44 mm women): baseline CT/CMR, TTE at 1 year, then every 2 to 3 years if growth under 3 mm/yr<br>- 45 to 49 mm: TTE yearly (6-monthly if growth 3 mm/yr or more)<br>- 50 to 52 mm ascending without high-risk features: CT/CMR at 6 months<br>- Not operated near threshold: re-image every 6 months<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Fig 24</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How is bicuspid aortopathy imaged?</summary><div class="fca">- CT or CMR of the whole thoracic aorta at diagnosis, when TTE disagrees, or above 45 mm (I C)<br>- TTE surveillance above 40 mm: at 1 year, then every 2 to 3 years if stable (I C)<br>- Screen first-degree relatives: root phenotype or isolated AR I C; all BAV IIa B<br>- ACC/AHA: lifelong surveillance even after AVR if 4.0 cm or more (1 B-NR)<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Table 68; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.1.3.1</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How are Marfan and Loeys-Dietz patients imaged?</summary><div class="fca">Marfan (ESC 2024): TTE at least yearly under 45 mm; 6-monthly under 45 mm with risk factors; 6 to 12-monthly at 45 mm or more; CMR/CT of whole aorta plus duplex at first visit and every 3 to 5 years (I C); after root replacement CMR at least every 3 years. ACC/AHA: TTE at diagnosis, 6 months, then yearly.<br>Loeys-Dietz: TTE every 6 to 12 months; baseline head-to-pelvis CMR/CT, then every 1 to 3 years (I C).<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Tables 60 and 65; ACC/AHA 2022</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What imaging follows root or ascending replacement?</summary><div class="fca">- TTE before discharge and at 1 month<br>- Fully repaired aorta: CT/CMR at 2 years, then every 5 years<br>- After open thoracic aortic repair: CT within 1 month, yearly for 2 years, then every 5 years (I B)<br>- Echo after every root operation (EACTS/STS, recommended)<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Fig 25 and follow-up table; EACTS/STS 2024</small></div></details>

## 12. Medical therapy

<details class="fc"><summary>What blood pressure target and agents are used in thoracic aneurysm?</summary><div class="fca">- ESC 2024: systolic 120 to 129 mmHg if tolerated (I A); ACE inhibitor or ARB first line in hypertensive aortic patients (IIa B); beta-blocker and/or ARB reasonable in TAA (data mostly Marfan)<br>- ACC/AHA 2022: target 130/80 or lower; beta-blocker to reach target (2a C-LD); ARB as adjunct (2a C-EO)<br>- Beta-blockers cut dP/dt and the rate of dilatation (TSRA)<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 sections 7 and 9.2.4; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.4.1; TSRA ch. 77</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What is the evidence and class for beta-blockers and ARBs in Marfan?</summary><div class="fca">- Beta-blocker or ARB at maximum tolerated dose: I A (ESC 2024; ACC/AHA 2022)<br>- Both together: ESC IIa A; ACC/AHA 2a C-LD<br>- RCTs: ARB equal to beta-blocker; adding ARB slowed root growth<br>- Avoid calcium-channel blockers in Marfan<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Table 61; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.1.2.3</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What other drug advice applies (statins, fluoroquinolones, vEDS, LDS)?</summary><div class="fca">- Statins for TAA growth and rupture may be considered: IIb B (ESC 2024)<br>- Smoking cessation: I A<br>- Fluoroquinolones may be used with a compelling indication and no alternative: IIb B<br>- Loeys-Dietz: beta-blocker and/or ARB, 2a C-EO (ACC/AHA)<br>- vEDS: celiprolol, IIa B (ESC)<br>- Limit isometric exercise; avoid cocaine and stimulants<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Tables 37 and 59; ACC/AHA 2022</small></div></details>

## 13. Women and pregnancy

<details class="fc"><summary>How does aortic disease differ in women?</summary><div class="fca">- Smaller normal diameters (root upper normal 34 to 36 mm vs 40 mm)<br>- TAA is 4 times commoner in men but grows faster in women (0.96 vs 0.45 mm/yr) and degenerative TAA carries 3 times the event risk<br>- Height-indexed thresholds may help in women (ACC/AHA)<br>- Women with TGFBR2 and small body size are high-risk<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 sections 5.4 and 9.2; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.5.1</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>When should a woman with Marfan have root surgery before pregnancy?</summary><div class="fca">- Over 45 mm: ESC 2024 I C; EACTS/STS 45 mm or more I C; ACC/AHA 1 C-LD<br>- 40 to 45 mm with risk factors (family history, growth): ESC IIb C; EACTS/STS IIb C; ACC/AHA 2b C-LD<br>Dissection risk about 1% at 40 mm or less; 40 to 45 mm is modified WHO class III, over 45 mm class IV.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Table 63; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 33; ACC/AHA 2022 Table 34; ESC/EACTS 2025 valve guideline</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Give other pre-pregnancy prophylactic surgery thresholds (ACC/AHA 2022).</summary><div class="fca">- Bicuspid valve 5.0 cm (1 C-EO; also ESC/EACTS 2025)<br>- Sporadic aneurysm 5.0 cm (1 C-EO)<br>- Non-syndromic HTAD 4.5 cm (1 C-EO)<br>- Loeys-Dietz TGFBR1, TGFBR2, SMAD3 4.0 cm (2b C-EO); TGFB2 or TGFB3 4.5 cm (2a C-EO)<br>- Turner ASI 2.5 cm/m² (1 C-LD; ESC: 25 mm/m²)<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ACC/AHA 2022 Table 34; ESC 2024 section 10.1.2; Cardiac 4 tab</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How should a woman with aortopathy deliver?</summary><div class="fca">- Aorta under 40 mm: vaginal (EACTS/STS I C; ACC/AHA 1 C-EO)<br>- 40 to 45 mm: vaginal with regional anaesthesia and assisted short second stage (2b); caesarean may be considered in HTAD<br>- 45 mm or more: caesarean (EACTS/STS IIa C; ACC/AHA 2a C-EO; ESC/EACTS 2025)<br>- Previous or chronic dissection: caesarean (I C)<br>- Deliver where cardiothoracic surgery is available (TSRA, I C)<br>- Ergometrine after delivery: not recommended (ESC 2025 pregnancy guideline III C, per Arbustini 2026 commentary)<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 33; ACC/AHA 2022 section 8.2; TSRA ch. 78; Arbustini et al. Eur Heart J Suppl 2026 (commentary on ESC 2025 pregnancy guideline)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How is acute type A dissection managed in pregnancy?</summary><div class="fca">- First or second trimester: urgent aortic repair with fetal monitoring (I C)<br>- Third trimester: urgent caesarean, then immediate aortic surgery (I C)<br>- Under 26 weeks: operate accepting fetal loss; over 28 weeks: deliver first<br>- Type B: medical therapy unless complicated (I C)<br>Risk peaks in the third trimester to 12 weeks postpartum.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 33; ACC/AHA 2022 section 8.4</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What drugs and imaging are used for aortopathy in pregnancy?</summary><div class="fca">- Beta-blocker through pregnancy and postpartum in HTAD (ESC I C; ACC/AHA 1 C-EO); labetalol suggested<br>- ARBs and ACE inhibitors contraindicated (ESC III B)<br>- TTE each trimester and postpartum (ACC/AHA 1 C-LD)<br>- Arch or distal aorta: MRI without gadolinium in preference to CT<br>- ESC 2025 pregnancy: ACE-I, ARB, ARNI, MRA, ivabradine, SGLT2i not recommended (III C); ergometrine after delivery III C<br>- Ionising imaging as low as reasonably achievable, ideally foetal dose under 50 mGy (ESC 2025 I C); never withhold imaging for suspected dissection<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 Rec Table 63; ACC/AHA 2022 section 8.1; Arbustini et al. Eur Heart J Suppl 2026 (commentary on ESC 2025 pregnancy guideline)</small></div></details>

## 14. Viva pitfalls

<details class="fc"><summary>Pitfall: gated CT shows sinuses 49 mm, tubular aorta 39 mm and moderate AR. Why not a tubular graft?</summary><div class="fca">This is a root phenotype, not a 49 mm ascending aneurysm. A supracoronary tube leaves the diseased sinuses and the mechanism of the AR. Root phenotype has a lower threshold (BAV 50 mm, I B).<small>Cardiac 6 tab (FRCS Atlas worked case)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Pitfall: an old non-gated CT said 45 mm; today&#x27;s gated CT says 47 mm. Has it grown?</summary><div class="fca">Growth is not proven. Re-measure the old images in the same plane and convention. An oblique axial cut can mimic growth, and inter-study variability is 2 to 3 mm. Do not mix EACTS/STS outer-edge and ESC inner-edge measurements.<small>Cardiac 6 tab (FRCS Atlas worked case; measuring section); Cardiac 9 tab</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Pitfall: a 46 mm Marfan root. &quot;It is below 55 mm, so watch it.&quot; What is wrong?</summary><div class="fca">The 55 mm threshold is for sporadic disease. Marfan: 50 mm (I), 45 mm with high-risk features (IIa), and before pregnancy over 45 mm (I). Loeys-Dietz TGFBR1/2: 45 mm (40 mm with high-risk features). Avoid TEVAR in native HTAD aorta.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 16; ESC 2024</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Pitfall: does AVR alone deal with bicuspid aortopathy?</summary><div class="fca">No. Follow the aorta for life after AVR if it is 4.0 cm or more (ACC/AHA 1 B-NR). After isolated AVR for BAV, 15-year freedom from aortic events was 85% with baseline dilatation vs 93% without. Replace at AVR if 45 mm or more.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ACC/AHA 2022 section 6.1.3</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Pitfall: after root replacement in Marfan, is the aortic risk gone?</summary><div class="fca">No. Type B dissection now exceeds type A in Marfan, often below thresholds and after root replacement. Distal dissection risk persists in pregnancy. Image the remaining aorta (CMR at least every 3 years, ESC).<small>Cardiac 6 tab; ESC 2024 section 10.1.3; ACC/AHA 2022</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Pitfall: &quot;The cusps look perfect for valve-sparing, so let&#x27;s operate on this 44 mm root.&quot;</summary><div class="fca">Keep three decisions separate: is repair indicated, how much aorta to replace, can the valve be kept. Favourable valve-sparing anatomy does not justify operating on a small low-risk root. Consent for the fallback (composite graft) and agree beforehand what converts the plan.<small>Cardiac 6 tab (FRCS Atlas draft, not checked against a primary source)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Pitfall: new regional wall motion abnormality after reimplantation, valve competent. What do you think of?</summary><div class="fca">Coronary button distortion or compromised coronary inflow. A competent valve does not clear the coronary reconstruction.<small>Cardiac 6 tab (FRCS Atlas worked case)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Pitfall: &quot;Diameter is a good predictor of type A dissection.&quot;</summary><div class="fca">It has good specificity but poor sensitivity (about 4%). Most type A dissections occur below 55 mm, and over 60% of non-Marfan, non-BAV cases arose in an undilated aorta. Weigh length, phenotype, height, family history, genetics, growth and aortic pain.<small>Cardiac 6 tab; EACTS/STS 2024 Rec Table 13 text; ESC 2024</small></div></details>
