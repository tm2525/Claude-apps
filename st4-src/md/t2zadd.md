## Fitness for lung resection: additions from the FRCS Atlas HTML builds
Added 20 November 2026 after comparing the FRCS Atlas HTML builds (53-chapter merger ch. 32, section 3 and worked case; 42-chapter merger ch. 25, section 4; Thoracic-and-Congenital "Lung cancer fitness and parenchymal preservation"; index_5 "Lung resection fitness and prehabilitation"; Coverage-and-Study "Lung cancer staging and resection"; Consolidated ch. 28) with this tab. Only points not already in the ST4 course are listed; each says whether it was checked against a primary source.

### Stair climb: 22 m or 12 m?
This tab notes that the slide archive's 12 m stair-climb band differs from the ERS/ESTS figures and calls the difference unresolved.
- Both Atlas chapters give 22 m: a climb of 22 m or more (or a shuttle walk over 400 m) is low risk; below that, formal CPET.
- TSRA Review 3rd ed., ch. 4 (PDF p. 64) agrees:
  - A patient who climbs 22 m of stairs (about 6 to 7 flights) has a VO2max over 15 mL/kg/min.
  - Over 15 mL/kg/min carries no increased risk of pulmonary complications or death; under 10 carries very high risk.
- The ERS/ESTS algorithm reproduced in BJA Education 2019 (Thoracic 17) also uses 22 m: under 22 m, CPET is highly recommended.
- So three sources use 22 m and only the slide archive uses 12 m. Checked: TSRA ch. 4, p. 64; BJA Education 2019;19:206-211, Fig. 1.

Further points from TSRA ch. 4 (PDF p. 64), not yet in this tab:
- DLCO correlates better with postoperative death than FEV1.
- Predicted postoperative FEV1 or DLCO under 40% is very high risk, and non-operative treatment should be considered. This matches the under-40% tier quoted from the ERS/ESTS 2025 slides.

### Thoracic Revised Cardiac Risk Index (ThRCRI)
The 42-chapter build gives the weights (FRCS Atlas draft; not checked against a primary source: none of the available guidelines contains the index):
| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Ischaemic heart disease | 1.5 |
| Cerebrovascular disease | 1.5 |
| Creatinine over 2 mg/dL | 1.0 |
| Planned pneumonectomy | 1.5 |
- A score of 2 or more warrants cardiology work-up, by the Atlas's statement. NICE NG122 instead uses "two or fewer risk factors with good functional capacity: no further tests", already in this tab.

### How firm are the ERS/ESTS 2025 tiers?
- The Atlas (Thoracic-and-Congenital build) states that the recommendations to calculate predicted postoperative values are conditional. It also states that the 60%, 40-59% and under-40% bands are risk strata, not eligibility cut-offs, to be combined with exercise testing, frailty, cardiac risk and the change after induction treatment. (FRCS Atlas draft; not checked: the 2025 guideline is not among the available sources)

### Worked case (Atlas 53-chapter build; arithmetic rechecked)
68-year-old smoker; 2.8 cm right upper lobe adenocarcinoma, cT1cN0M0; FEV1 65%, DLCO 52%.
1. Right upper lobectomy removes 3 of 19 segments, so multiply by 16/19 (0.842): ppoFEV1 54.7%, ppoDLCO 43.8%.
2. Under the ERS/ESTS 2025 tiers in this tab, ppoDLCO 43.8% is moderate risk (40-59%), so do a stair climb or shuttle walk. In the older 30-60% band (Thoracic 17) the step is the same.
3. CPET shows peak VO2 15.5 mL/kg/min. This tab's table places 15 to 20 mL/kg/min at low to intermediate risk, where lobectomy is generally appropriate. The Atlas's model answer is VATS or robotic right upper lobectomy with prehabilitation, smoking cessation and enhanced recovery.
