## 1. Principles and timing

<details class="fc"><summary>Name the three indications for surgery in active left-sided infective endocarditis.</summary><div class="fca">- Heart failure (commonest)<br>- Uncontrolled infection (second commonest)<br>- Prevention of septic embolism, especially to the CNS<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 indications and timing section); ESC 2023 section 8; TSRA ch. 67</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How does ESC 2023 define emergency, urgent and non-urgent surgery in IE?</summary><div class="fca">- Emergency: within 24 h, irrespective of how long antibiotics have been given<br>- Urgent: within 3 to 5 days<br>- Non-urgent: within the same hospital admission<br>If antibiotics alone cure the infection, residual valve disease follows the ordinary valve guidelines.<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 indications and timing section); ESC 2023 section 8</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Pitfall: what does &#x27;urgent&#x27; mean in the ESC 2023 algorithm for surgery after stroke (Figure 11)?</summary><div class="fca">Within 48 to 72 h, not the 3 to 5 days of the main timing definitions. Say which definition you are using.<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 indications and surgical principles sections); ESC 2023 Figure 11</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Why is early surgery most useful for preventing embolism?</summary><div class="fca">Embolic risk is highest the day after antibiotics start: 10 to 20 times higher around the start than at 2 weeks, then it falls steadily over the first 2 weeks.<br>- Embolic events in 20 to 50%<br>- Up to 50% of emboli are clinically silent<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 indications section 8.4); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What does surgery gain, and which patients do worst?</summary><div class="fca">- Survival advantage of up to 20% in the first year; in-hospital mortality after IE surgery 10 to 20%, higher over 75<br>- Worst prognosis: a clear indication that is not operated on (in PVE, withholding indicated surgery is the biggest risk factor for recurrence and death)<br>- The elderly are under-offered surgery (EURO-ENDO, over 80 v under 80: operated when indicated 35% v 68%, matched surgical mortality 19.7% v 20.0%)<br>- A decision not to operate belongs to the Endocarditis Team, with experienced surgical input<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 indications, follow-up and PVE sections); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How do AHA 2026 and AATS 2016 put timing once surgery is indicated?</summary><div class="fca">- AHA 2026: early surgery within 48 to 72 h unless there has been a major stroke or cerebral haemorrhage. Antibiotics need not be completed first (EASE)<br>- AATS 2016: operate within days (I). The patient should be on appropriate antibiotics at surgery (I), and further delay after that is unlikely to help (IIa)<br>- ESC 2023 for comparison: emergency within 24 h, urgent 3 to 5 days, non-urgent in the same admission<small>Cardiac 8 (AHA 2026 part 4; AATS 2016 overview and surgical approach sections); AHA 2026; AATS 2016</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>TSRA: what are the indications for emergent surgery and for early surgery (within 48 to 72 h)?</summary><div class="fca">- Emergent: severe acute AI with early mitral valve closure; rupture into the pericardium or a heart chamber<br>- Early (48 to 72 h): left-sided vegetation &gt;10 mm (higher stroke risk)<br>- Conflict: TSRA lists fungal IE and recurrent emboli as &#x27;elective&#x27;. ESC 2023 makes fungi urgent or non-urgent by haemodynamic state (I C), and vegetation ≥10 mm with embolism urgent (I B)<small>TSRA ch. 67 (American classes as quoted); Cardiac 8 (TSRA section)</small></div></details>

## 2. Heart failure

<details class="fc"><summary>Give the ESC 2023 emergency indication for surgery in left-sided IE, with its class and level.</summary><div class="fca">Aortic or mitral NVE or PVE with severe acute regurgitation, obstruction or fistula causing refractory pulmonary oedema or cardiogenic shock: emergency surgery (within 24 h), I B.<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 12); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Give the ESC 2023 urgent heart-failure indication, and the echo signs of poor haemodynamic tolerance.</summary><div class="fca">Severe acute regurgitation or obstruction causing HF symptoms or echo signs of poor haemodynamic tolerance: urgent, I B.<br>Echo signs: raised LVEDP, high LA pressure, moderate to severe pulmonary hypertension, new raised filling pressures, pericardial effusion.<br>Without haemodynamic compromise: antibiotics and close surveillance. Early surgery is still a good option if surgical risk is low.<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 indications section 8.2); ESC 2023 Rec Table 12</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Heart failure in IE: how common is it, and what are the mechanisms?</summary><div class="fca">- The most frequent complication and the main indication for emergency and urgent surgery; 19 to 73% of left-sided IE<br>- Cardiogenic shock is the first presentation in up to 5%<br>- Mechanisms: leaflet perforation or rupture, mitral chordal rupture; less often fistula, vegetation obstructing a leaflet, coronary embolism<br>- Surgery is the only treatment shown to improve survival; the benefit is greatest in NYHA III to IV<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 indications section 8.2); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Pitfall: should emergency surgery wait for the Endocarditis Team meeting, more antibiotics or coronary imaging?</summary><div class="fca">No. Operate for new NYHA IV, pulmonary oedema or shock regardless of infection status or how long antibiotics have run, unless it would be futile. Team discussion must not delay emergency surgery. In an emergency, operate without coronary assessment (IIa C).<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 indications and surgical principles sections); ESC 2023 Rec Table 16</small></div></details>

## 3. Uncontrolled infection

<details class="fc"><summary>What counts as locally uncontrolled infection, and what does ESC 2023 recommend?</summary><div class="fca">Abscess, false aneurysm, fistula, enlarging vegetation, prosthetic dehiscence, new AV block: urgent surgery, I B.<br>Rarely, a small abscess or pseudoaneurysm can be treated medically if there is no other indication and the fever is easily controlled.<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 12, section 8.3); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Persistent bacteraemia: what is the definition, what is the ESC 2023 class, and what must you exclude first?</summary><div class="fca">- Definition: fever and positive cultures after 7 days of appropriate treatment<br>- Cultures positive &gt;1 week, or persistent sepsis despite antibiotics and control of metastatic foci: urgent surgery should be considered, IIa B<br>- Pitfall: the Atlas draft lists this as Class I, which is wrong<br>- First exclude extracardiac abscess (splenic, vertebral, cerebral, renal), infected lines, other foci and drug fever<br>- Older and American thresholds: AHA 2022 says 5 to 7 days; AATS 2016 says &gt;5 to 7 days (I B); TSRA says sepsis persisting 72 h<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 12; Atlas additions; AHA 2022; Sidik 2025 table); ESC 2023; TSRA ch. 67</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>ESC 2023: when should IE due to fungi or multiresistant organisms be operated on?</summary><div class="fca">Urgent or non-urgent according to the haemodynamic state, I C.<br>Figure 10: urgent for S. aureus (MR or MS); non-urgent for other resistant bacteria (VRE, non-HACEK Gram-negatives) and fungi.<br>Pitfall: the Atlas draft says &#x27;always urgent&#x27;, which is wrong.<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 12, Figure 10; Atlas additions); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Staphylococcal and Gram-negative PVE: what is the ESC 2023 position, and why is S. aureus treated as a resistant organism?</summary><div class="fca">- PVE due to S. aureus or non-HACEK Gram-negatives: urgent surgery should be considered, IIa C<br>- S. aureus destroys tissue quickly, especially when the early response is poor: discuss in the team and operate urgently<br>- Caution: early surgery in S. aureus IE has been linked to prosthetic dehiscence<br>- AATS 2016 and AHA 2022: left-sided S. aureus is itself an indication for early surgery<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 12, section 8.3; AHA 2022; Sidik 2025 AATS column); ESC 2023; AATS 2016</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Septic shock in IE: how is it defined, how common is it, and how is it managed?</summary><div class="fca">- Vasopressor needed to keep MAP ≥65 mmHg, with lactate &gt;2 mmol/L, without hypovolaemia; about 5 to 10%<br>- Urgent surgery is recommended when not futile, because surgery lowers early and 1-year mortality<br>- Vasoplegia on bypass: noradrenaline first, then vasopressin or terlipressin, with methylene blue as rescue<br>- Haemoadsorption: no benefit in an RCT<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 sections 8.3 and 10.2); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Give the key viva facts on fungal endocarditis.</summary><div class="fca">- Mostly PVE, PWID and the immunocompromised; Candida and Aspergillus (Aspergillus gives culture-negative IE)<br>- Mortality &gt;50%; combination antifungals and a low threshold for surgery (ESC 2023)<br>- TSRA: the only proven cure is surgical excision of the valve with amphotericin B<br>- AHA 2026: fungal PVE relapses in up to 36%; oral azole suppression, sometimes lifelong<br>- PWID using brown heroin in lemon juice with very large vegetations: think non-albicans Candida<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 antimicrobial part 2 and right-sided sections; AHA 2026 part 4); TSRA ch. 67</small></div></details>

## 4. Perivalvular extension

<details class="fc"><summary>Perivalvular extension: how common is it, and where does it spread?</summary><div class="fca">- 10 to 30% of NVE, more in PVE; commoner in aortic than mitral IE (possibly more with bicuspid valves)<br>- Aortic IE extends most often into the mitral-aortic intervalvular fibrosa<br>- Mitral abscesses are usually posterior or lateral; mitral annular calcification can hide them<br>- Clues: persistent fever, new AV block, chest pain, new murmur, recurrent embolism, HF<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 section 8.3); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Why does new AV block matter in IE?</summary><div class="fca">- The AV node and His bundle lie by the septal tricuspid leaflet, below the non-coronary and right coronary cusps, and near the mitral annulus. New block suggests paravalvular extension, especially aortic<br>- New AV block from an abscess means urgent surgery (I B, locally uncontrolled infection)<br>- EURO-ENDO: a conduction abnormality at diagnosis in 11.5% (complete block 2.8%)<br>- TSRA: ECG conduction changes are an urgent indication<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 section 9.5); ESC 2023; TSRA ch. 67</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How is a pseudoaneurysm told apart from an abscess on echo, and where can it rupture?</summary><div class="fca">An echo-free perivalvular cavity that communicates with the bloodstream: it expands in systole and collapses in diastole. It is usually a root abscess that has progressed.<br>It can rupture into the LA, aorta, LV or pericardium (fistula). 3D TOE shows the rupture site and its relation to the coronary origins.<small>Cardiac 8 (Baltodano-Arellano 2024 section)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How well do TTE, TOE and cardiac CT detect a perivalvular abscess?</summary><div class="fca">- TTE: sensitivity 28%, specificity 98%<br>- 2D TOE: 87% and 95%<br>- AHA 2026, TOE v CT sensitivity: abscess or pseudoaneurysm 69% v 78% (CT better); vegetation 94% v 64% (TOE better)<br>- ESC 2023: cardiac CTA for paravalvular complications when echo is inconclusive, I B<small>Cardiac 8 (Baltodano-Arellano 2024; AHA 2026 part 1); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>When should an epicardial pacemaker be placed at IE surgery?</summary><div class="fca">Should be considered (IIa C) for complete AV block at surgery with any predictor that it will persist:<br>- Pre-operative conduction abnormality<br>- S. aureus<br>- Aortic root abscess<br>- Tricuspid involvement<br>- Previous valve surgery<br>12.8% of 444 survivors needed a pacemaker.<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 14); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

## 5. Embolism and vegetation size

<details class="fc"><summary>Give the three ESC 2023 indications for surgery to prevent embolism, with class and level.</summary><div class="fca">All urgent:<br>- Aortic or mitral NVE or PVE with a persistent vegetation ≥10 mm after one or more embolic episodes despite appropriate antibiotics: I B<br>- Vegetation ≥10 mm with another indication for surgery: I C<br>- Aortic or mitral vegetation ≥10 mm without severe valve dysfunction or clinical embolism, at low surgical risk: IIb B<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 12); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How did the vegetation-size indications change from ESC 2015 to ESC 2023?</summary><div class="fca">- Isolated vegetation: &gt;15 mm, IIb C (2015) became ≥10 mm at low risk, IIb B (2023)<br>- Vegetation &gt;10 mm with severe valve dysfunction at low risk: IIa B (2015) became ≥10 mm with any other indication, I C (2023)<br>- 2015 had isolated very large vegetation &gt;30 mm as urgent IIa B. In 2023, &gt;30 mm is described as very high neurological risk<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 new and revised recommendations, Table 4; Sidik 2025 quoting ESC 2015); ESC 2023; ESC 2015</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Which factors predict embolism in IE?</summary><div class="fca">- Vegetation size and mobility (the most important)<br>- Mitral site, especially the anterior leaflet<br>- Growth on treatment<br>- Organism: S. aureus, S. gallolyticus, Candida<br>- Previous embolism, multivalvular IE<br>Size &gt;10 mm v &lt;10 mm: embolism OR 2.28, mortality OR 1.63 (meta-analysis of 21 studies).<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 section 8.4; surgical approach section citing Open Heart 2025); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How should vegetation size be measured?</summary><div class="fca">By maximal length, on multiplanar reconstruction, not on volume rendering (its spatial resolution is lower). 3D lengths are longer: in one study the best embolism cut-off was 17 mm on 3D against 15 mm on 2D (accuracy 78% v 65%), so 3D would send more patients to surgery.<small>Cardiac 8 (Baltodano-Arellano 2024; ESC 2023 thresholds); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Describe the EASE trial and its limitations.</summary><div class="fca">Kang, NEJM 2012: 76 patients with left-sided NVE, severe valve disease and vegetation &gt;10 mm, randomised to surgery within 48 h or conventional care.<br>- In-hospital death or embolism at 6 weeks: 3% v 23%; 6-month composite 3% v 28%<br>- No difference in 6-month all-cause mortality<br>- Limits: small, single country, young, low risk, mostly streptococcal. It supports early surgery for a large vegetation with severe valve disease, not for every vegetation &gt;10 mm<small>Cardiac 8 (AATS 2016 overview, trials); archcardiac; ESC 2023 section 8.4</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What is the significance of a vegetation &gt;30 mm, and how does AHA 2026 define high re-embolism risk?</summary><div class="fca">- &gt;30 mm: neurological risk particularly high (ESC 2023). ESC 2015 made it urgent IIa B<br>- AHA 2026 high re-embolism risk: large or mobile vegetation &gt;10 mm, mitral involvement (especially the anterior leaflet), staphylococcal IE<br>- AHA 2026 indication: vegetation &gt;1 cm threatening embolism<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 section 8.4; AHA 2026 part 4; Sidik 2025); ESC 2023; AHA 2026</small></div></details>

## 6. Neurological complications

<details class="fc"><summary>ESC 2023: when should you operate after a TIA or an ischaemic stroke?</summary><div class="fca">- TIA: operate without delay, I B<br>- Stroke: operate without delay if there is HF, uncontrolled infection, abscess or persistent high embolic risk, provided there is no coma and haemorrhage is excluded on CT or MRI, I B<br>- Why: haemorrhagic conversion of an uncomplicated ischaemic lesion is less likely than recurrent embolism<br>- Post-operative haemorrhagic conversion after prior stroke: 2 to 7%, with mortality about 40%<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 17, section 10.4); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>ESC 2023: what is the timing of surgery after an intracranial haemorrhage?</summary><div class="fca">- Delay surgery &gt;1 month if possible, with frequent clinical and imaging reassessment: IIa C<br>- With instability (HF, uncontrolled infection, persistent high embolic risk): consider urgent or emergency surgery, weighing the chance of a meaningful neurological outcome: IIa C (new in 2023)<br>- If surgery is delayed, repeat CT or MRI 1 to 2 weeks after the haemorrhage<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 17); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>ESC 2023 Figure 11: which features favour urgent surgery after a haemorrhagic stroke, and which point to palliation?</summary><div class="fca">- Favourable: haemorrhage volume &lt;30 mL or NIHSS &lt;12, so urgent surgery (I). Otherwise reassess often and delay 4 weeks if possible<br>- Conservative or palliative care: GCS ≤4 or NIHSS &gt;18, or no prospect of meaningful recovery<br>- Cardiogenic shock: emergency surgery (I)<br>- Step 1 is always neurology or neurosurgery assessment plus Endocarditis Team discussion (I)<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Figure 11); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Compare the guidelines on delaying surgery after an intracranial haemorrhage or a major stroke.</summary><div class="fca">Newest first:<br>- AHA 2026: haemorrhagic or major ischaemic stroke, at least 2 weeks, preferably 4. Small infarct with no deficit, no delay. Coma, surgery not appropriate<br>- ESC 2023: ICH &gt;1 month (IIa C); no delay for ischaemic stroke<br>- AATS 2016: ≥3 weeks after ICH (IIa)<br>- TSRA: extensive damage or ICH, wait ≥4 weeks (IIb)<br>- Sidik 2025: major ischaemic stroke or any ICH, ≥4 weeks<small>Cardiac 8 (AHA 2026 part 4; ESC 2023 Rec Table 17; AATS 2016; Sidik 2025); Cardiac (timing section); TSRA ch. 67</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>AHA 2026: how should an embolic stroke be handled in an unstable patient or one at high re-embolism risk?</summary><div class="fca">- Embolic stroke without haemorrhage, with instability (acute HF, uncontrolled sepsis, abscess) or high re-embolism risk: consider urgent (&lt;72 h) or emergency surgery<br>- Embolic stroke with intracranial haemorrhage: early surgery in selected high-risk or unstable cases, otherwise defer 3 to 4 weeks<br>- Exclude a mycotic aneurysm (CTA or MRA) before cardiac surgery<small>Cardiac 8 (AHA 2026 part 4); AHA 2026</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Do cerebral microbleeds or silent emboli delay surgery?</summary><div class="fca">No.<br>- Microbleeds (on MRI in 50 to 60% of IE) are not linked to parenchymal haemorrhage or post-operative neurological complications. They should not postpone surgery and are not a minor criterion<br>- Silent emboli on DWI count as a minor criterion but are no reason to delay (AHA 2026)<br>- Silent cerebral lesions in up to 80%; symptomatic in up to 35%<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 section 9.1; AHA 2026 part 1); ESC 2023; AHA 2026</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Should thrombolysis or thrombectomy be used for a stroke in IE?</summary><div class="fca">- Thrombolysis: not recommended, III C (AHA 2026: 20% higher mortality from haemorrhage)<br>- Mechanical thrombectomy: may be considered if the expertise is available in time, IIb C. Send the retrieved material for pathology and microbiology<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 13; AHA 2026 part 4); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Infective (mycotic) aneurysm: how is it imaged, and how is treatment sequenced with valve surgery?</summary><div class="fca">- CT or MR angiography if suspected (I B); invasive angiography if those are negative and suspicion remains (IIa B); DSA is the gold standard<br>- CTA sensitivity: 57% for aneurysms &lt;5 mm, 94% for ≥5 mm<br>- Treat large, growing or ruptured aneurysms (I C)<br>- Cardiac surgery can follow endovascular treatment on the same day; clipping usually needs ≥2 weeks before cardiac surgery<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 section 9.2, Rec Table 13); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Which features favour a non-mechanical valve in surgery for acute IE (ESC 2023 Table 12)?</summary><div class="fca">- Early surgery after a recent ischaemic stroke<br>- Intracranial bleeding<br>- Woman of childbearing age; advanced age or frailty<br>- Likely prolonged mechanical circulatory support or a complicated post-operative course<br>- Poor or unknown compliance; patient preference<br>AATS 2016: avoiding a mechanical valve after stroke or intracranial bleeding is reasonable (IIa).<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Table 12; AATS 2016); ESC 2023; AATS 2016</small></div></details>

## 7. Prosthetic valve endocarditis

<details class="fc"><summary>What is early PVE, and what does ESC 2023 recommend for it?</summary><div class="fca">PVE within 6 months of valve surgery: repeat surgery with a new valve replacement and complete debridement, I C (new in 2023).<br>Antibiotics alone rarely cure it. Usual organisms are staphylococci and Cutibacterium.<br>The empirical antibiotic tables use &lt;12 months instead.<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 19, section 12.1); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>PVE: what are the incidence and in-hospital mortality, and how often is it operated on when indicated?</summary><div class="fca">- 1 to 6% of prosthesis recipients; 0.3 to 1.2% per patient-year; 20 to 30% of all IE (31% in EURO-ENDO)<br>- In-hospital mortality 20 to 40%<br>- Only 73% of patients with a clear indication had surgery (EURO-ENDO)<br>- TSRA: PVE in 1 to 4% in the first year; risk about 1% in year 1 after SAVR, 0.6 to 3.4% after TAVI<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 section 12.1); ESC 2023; TSRA ch. 67</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Which PVE can be treated without surgery, and which needs surgery?</summary><div class="fca">- Conservative: uncomplicated, non-staphylococcal late PVE, with close follow-up<br>- Surgery: HF, severe prosthetic dysfunction, abscess, persistent fever; early PVE (I C); S. aureus or non-HACEK Gram-negative PVE (IIa C)<br>- TSRA: PVE relapsing after a completed course, surgery Class I<br>- At operation remove the prosthesis, sutures and pledgets<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 section 12.1); ESC 2023; TSRA ch. 67</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Why is PVE harder to diagnose, and what does imaging add?</summary><div class="fca">- Echo and blood cultures are each only about 60% sensitive; persistent post-operative fever should raise suspicion<br>- A new periprosthetic leak is a major criterion<br>- FDG-PET/CT and cardiac CTA in possible PVE: I B (PET sensitivity 86%, specificity 84%)<br>- Pitfall: abnormal periprosthetic uptake is major whatever the time since surgery. The Atlas draft&#x27;s &#x27;over 3 months&#x27; is wrong<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 section 12.1; Atlas additions); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What are the key numbers for TAVI endocarditis?</summary><div class="fca">- 0.3 to 1.9 per 100 patient-years; enterococci are the leading cause (AHA 2026)<br>- No fever in 13 to 20%; no vegetation on TOE in 38 to 60%<br>- 30-day mortality 16 to 36%; 1-year 41 to 59%<br>- Surgery in only about 20%; benefit shown mainly with local extension (abscess, fistula)<br>- Valve-in-valve for healed IE: at least 1 to 3 months after the episode, with a negative TOE<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 section 12.3; AHA 2026 part 1); ESC 2023; AHA 2026</small></div></details>

## 8. Right-sided IE and people who inject drugs

<details class="fc"><summary>ESC 2023 Table 21: what are the indications for surgery in right-sided IE, with class and level?</summary><div class="fca">On appropriate antibiotics:<br>- RV dysfunction from acute severe TR not responding to diuretics: I B<br>- Persistent vegetation with respiratory failure needing ventilation after recurrent PE: I B<br>- Residual tricuspid vegetation &gt;20 mm after recurrent septic PE: I C<br>- Simultaneous left-heart involvement: I C<br>- Persistent bacteraemia or sepsis after ≥1 week of antibiotics: IIa C<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 21); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Pitfall: are the right-sided indications one combined criterion, and is a large vegetation alone an indication?</summary><div class="fca">- They are separate indications, and any one is enough (course pack correction)<br>- An isolated vegetation is not an indication<br>- About 90% of right-sided IE is cured medically; only 5 to 40% of non-complex cases are operated on (AHA 2022)<small>Cardiac 8 (thresholds; ESC 2023 right-sided section; AHA 2022); Cardiac (course pack corrections)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What are the AHA 2026 indications for right-sided surgery, and how do they compare with ESC?</summary><div class="fca">AHA 2026:<br>- Persistent bacteraemia<br>- Ongoing septic pulmonary emboli<br>- Lung abscess after 7 days of antibiotics<br>- Right-sided vegetation &gt;2 cm<br>Repair is preferred. The &gt;2 cm threshold is the same as ESC 2023 (&gt;20 mm) and de Waha 2026.<small>Cardiac 8 (AHA 2026 part 4); Cardiac 2 (tricuspid endocarditis, AHA statements); AHA 2026</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What are the technique recommendations for tricuspid IE?</summary><div class="fca">- Repair rather than replace when possible: IIa B<br>- If replaced: usually a bioprosthesis<br>- Prophylactic epicardial pacing lead at tricuspid surgery: IIa C<br>- Aspiration debulking of right-sided septic masses at high surgical risk: IIb C<br>- TSRA: De Vega annuloplasty and leaflet patching; some centres excise and replace later<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Table 3 and Rec Table 21); ESC 2023; TSRA ch. 67</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What do the sources say about valvectomy for tricuspid IE?</summary><div class="fca">- AHA 2022: not recommended (significant morbidity)<br>- ESC 2023, de Waha 2026, Luc 2019: for selected patients, as a staged first operation<br>- Luc (752 patients), valvectomy v replacement: 30-day mortality 13% v 7%, right HF 27% v 11%, reoperation 56% v 14%, recurrent IE 7% v 19%. Only prolonged ventilation differed significantly<br>- In a viva, state both positions and which one you follow<small>Cardiac 8 (AHA 2022 section; PWID tricuspid strategy section); Cardiac 2 (Luc 2019)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>When is a 2-week antibiotic regimen acceptable in right-sided IE?</summary><div class="fca">Oxacillin or cloxacillin without gentamicin, if all of these apply:<br>- MSSA, with good clinical and microbiological response (&gt;96 h)<br>- Vegetation ≤20 mm<br>- No metastatic infection, empyema or complications<br>- No prosthetic or left-sided valve infection, no severe immunosuppression<br>No glycopeptides. AHA 2022 does not advocate routine use.<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 right-sided section; AHA 2022); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>PWID: what else must the management plan include?</summary><div class="fca">- Addiction treatment after PWID-related IE: I C (start buprenorphine or methadone during the admission)<br>- OPAT not recommended: III C<br>- Recurrence is much higher, especially in the first 6 months after surgery; continued injecting in up to 70%<br>- A repeat operation is a team decision and needs an addiction plan (AHA 2022)<br>- Redo 30-day mortality 8.1% v 4.8% first time (Mori 2020, OR 2.22)<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 18; AHA 2022; de Waha 2026; Mori 2020); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What is the role of percutaneous aspiration (AngioVac), and what are its limits?</summary><div class="fca">- Debulking for source control of right-sided or lead vegetations in high surgical risk patients (ESC IIb C); used mostly in PWID and lead infection with S. aureus<br>- It does not treat valve dysfunction, and TR can get worse<br>- The evidence is observational, and the Endocarditis Team decides<small>Cardiac 8 (AngioVac section; AHA 2026 part 4); Cardiac 2 (AHA statements)</small></div></details>

## 9. Device infection

<details class="fc"><summary>ESC 2023: how is definite CIED-related IE managed?</summary><div class="fca">- Complete system extraction without delay, on initial empirical antibiotics: I B<br>- At least 3 blood culture sets, then cover MRSA and Gram-negatives: I C<br>- Percutaneous extraction is preferred, in an expert centre with surgical back-up<br>- Surgical extraction for large vegetations (e.g. &gt;20 mm) if aspiration is unavailable or fails, and preferred if valve surgery is indicated<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 CIED section, Rec Table 20); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Valve IE in a patient with a pacemaker but no lead involvement: should the device come out?</summary><div class="fca">- ESC 2023: complete extraction should be considered (IIa C), depending on the pathogen and the need for valve surgery<br>- AHA 2026: extraction is preferred even without lead vegetation. At valve surgery remove the generator and leads; place an epicardial system then, or a new device later<br>- AATS 2016: remove the whole system when device infection is likely (I); reasonable for S. aureus or fungi even without proof (IIa)<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 20; AHA 2026 part 4; AATS 2016 surgical approach); ESC 2023; AHA 2026; AATS 2016</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>CIED: what should be done after a single positive blood culture, or with occult bacteraemia?</summary><div class="fca">- Single positive culture with no other evidence of infection: do not remove, III C<br>- Occult Gram-positive bacteraemia or fungaemia persisting after a course of antibiotics: removal IIa C<br>- Occult Gram-negative bacteraemia, persistent or relapsing: removal IIb C<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 20); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>CIED: how long are antibiotics given after extraction, and when can a device be reimplanted?</summary><div class="fca">- No vegetation, not S. aureus: 2 weeks (IIb C)<br>- Vegetation or S. aureus: 4 weeks (IIa)<br>- Septic emboli or a prosthetic valve: 4 to 6 weeks (IIa C)<br>- Reimplant at a distant site once cultures are negative ≥72 h (no vegetations or ghosts), or after ≥2 weeks if vegetations were seen (I C)<br>- Pacing-dependent: active-fixation lead via the internal jugular to an external box, for up to 4 to 6 weeks<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 CIED section, Figure 13, Rec Table 20); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

## 10. Diagnosis: ESC 2023 and Duke-ISCVID

<details class="fc"><summary>ESC 2023 modified criteria: what defines definite and possible IE?</summary><div class="fca">- Definite: 2 major; or 1 major + 3 or more minor; or 5 minor<br>- Possible: 1 major + 1 or 2 minor; or 3 or 4 minor<br>- Major: microbiology (typical organisms in 2 cultures, persistent bacteraemia, Coxiella) and imaging (echo, cardiac CT, FDG-PET/CT, WBC SPECT/CT)<br>- The old modified Duke criteria had 80% sensitivity<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 diagnostic criteria); ESC 2023 Table 10; TSRA ch. 67 (modified Duke)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What does Duke-ISCVID 2023 add or change compared with ESC 2023?</summary><div class="fca">- Molecular diagnostics (PCR, metagenomics) added to the pathological criteria<br>- New major surgical criterion: inspection of the infected valve at operation<br>- Blood culture timing and separate venepuncture sites no longer required; atypical organisms count if found in 3 or more cultures<br>- Echo minor criteria removed; a CIED is a minor criterion; WBC SPECT is not a criterion<br>- Similar sensitivity to ESC 2023, higher specificity<small>Cardiac 8 (AHA 2026 part 1 comparison table); AHA 2026</small></div></details>

## 11. Endocarditis Team

<details class="fc"><summary>ESC 2023: where should complicated IE be managed, and at what class?</summary><div class="fca">- Complicated IE: diagnosed and managed early in a Heart Valve Centre with immediate surgical facilities and an Endocarditis Team, I B (2015: IIa B)<br>- Uncomplicated IE in a Referring Centre: early, regular communication with the Heart Valve Centre, I B<br>- Any IE patient needing ICU: urgent Endocarditis Team discussion<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 4; new and revised recommendations); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Who are the core members of the Endocarditis Team, and who makes the final decision to operate?</summary><div class="fca">- ESC 2023 core: cardiologists, cardiac imaging experts, cardiovascular surgeons, infectious disease specialist, microbiologist, OPAT specialist. A weekly meeting is suggested<br>- Adjunct: neurology and neurosurgery, radiology and nuclear medicine, anaesthesia, ICU, nephrology, addiction medicine, CIED extraction, pathology<br>- AHA 2026: the team agrees indication and timing; the final decision to operate rests with the surgeon. Addiction medicine is essential for PWID<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 prevention and team section, Table 7; AHA 2026 parts 1 and 4); ESC 2023; AHA 2026</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How do you present an endocarditis case at the MDT?</summary><div class="fca">- Organism, source, days of effective antibiotics, and whether cultures are now negative<br>- Valve, native or prosthetic, and severity of dysfunction; haemodynamic state<br>- Local extension (abscess, pseudoaneurysm, fistula, new AV block); vegetation size and emboli<br>- Neurological status and brain imaging; operative risk, frailty, plan for injecting drug use<br>- Proposal: surgery and its timing, or medical therapy with a review date<small>Cardiac 8 (Endocarditis team meeting: course notes)</small></div></details>

## 12. Surgical principles

<details class="fc"><summary>ESC 2023: how are the coronaries assessed before IE surgery?</summary><div class="fca">- Stable, aortic vegetation, high CAD risk: coronary CTA, I B<br>- High CAD risk without aortic vegetation: invasive angiography, I C<br>- Emergency: no pre-operative coronary assessment, IIa C<br>- Invasive angiography despite an aortic vegetation in selected known CAD: IIb C<br>High risk: men &gt;40, post-menopausal women, 1 or more risk factors, or known CAD.<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 16); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What are the operative principles of surgery for IE?</summary><div class="fca">- Median sternotomy (AATS I); intra-operative TOE (ESC I C, AATS I)<br>- Minimal handling of the heart before the cross-clamp, to avoid embolising<br>- Radical debridement of all infected and necrotic tissue and foreign material (AATS I), never compromised<br>- Reconstruction sized to what is left; repair mitral and tricuspid disease limited to the leaflets (AATS I)<br>- Tissue for culture, histology and 16S PCR. TSRA: good exposure and 6 weeks of post-operative antibiotics<small>Cardiac 8 (AATS 2016 surgical approach; ESC 2023 section 10); TSRA ch. 67; AATS 2016</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>ESC 2023: how is an aortic root abscess managed?</summary><div class="fca">- Shallow annular defect (very limited abscess, small pseudoaneurysm): conventional valve replacement<br>- Extensive root abscess or periannular destruction: aortic root replacement. Allograft preferred in experienced centres, though no substitute is proven superior<br>- Patching abscess cavities to avoid radical resection is discouraged (recurrence, leak, pseudoaneurysm). Excluded cavities drain into the pericardium<br>- AATS 2016: allograft or biological root preferred to a prosthetic conduit when destruction goes beyond the annulus (IIa)<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 section 10.3; AATS 2016); ESC 2023; AATS 2016</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Conflict: how do TSRA and the course notes treat a root abscess, compared with ESC 2023?</summary><div class="fca">- TSRA: bovine patch repair of the annulus or sinus if up to one-third is involved; root replacement (homograft or biological Bentall) if more<br>- Course notes and Ikonomidis 2011: unroof and debride the cavity, close or patch it with pericardium, then replace the valve or root<br>- ESC 2023 and Sidik 2025: patching cavities instead of radical resection is discouraged<br>- Reconcile: a patch to rebuild the annulus after radical debridement is acceptable, never one that replaces the debridement<small>TSRA ch. 67; Cardiac (timing and operative strategy); Cardiac 8 (Ikonomidis 2011; Sidik 2025; ESC 2023 section 10.3)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Why is a homograft favoured for the destroyed aortic root?</summary><div class="fca">- It conforms to irregular surfaces, is haemostatic and has good haemodynamics<br>- Low thromboembolic and reinfection risk<br>- Its anterior mitral leaflet can rebuild an infected anterior mitral leaflet or the curtain<br>- Limits: availability and structural degeneration. Alternatives: a stentless root; Ross in very selected patients (children)<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 section 10.3; Ikonomidis 2011; Sidik 2025); Cardiac 4 (prosthesis choice); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Ikonomidis 2011: what is the operative ladder for aortic valve endocarditis?</summary><div class="fca">- Cusps only, annulus intact: valve replacement<br>- Abscess cavity found: debride, then close it with pledgeted sandwich sutures or a small bovine patch, then replace the valve<br>- More than one-third of the annulus or adjacent structures involved (usual in PVE): homograft root<br>- Posterior bleeding that cannot be reached: bovine pericardial baffle to the right atrium<small>Cardiac 8 (Ikonomidis 2011 section)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What is a Commando operation, and when is it used?</summary><div class="fca">Used when infection destroys the intervalvular fibrosa with both aortic and mitral involvement.<br>- Bicaval cannulation; aortotomy through the non-coronary sinus and annulus, the anterior mitral leaflet and onto the LA dome<br>- Radical debridement; mitral prosthesis sewn to the posterior annulus<br>- Bovine pericardial patch rebuilds the curtain and LA roof; the aortic valve is sewn to the annulus and patch<br>- Hemi-Commando: a homograft root with its anterior mitral leaflet rebuilds the curtain, and the native mitral valve is kept<small>TSRA ch. 67; Cardiac 8 (surgical approach; Perri and Johnston 2022; Navia 2023)</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What are the outcomes of the Commando operation?</summary><div class="fca">- David (182 patients, 1985 to 2020): operative mortality 13.2%; 10-year survival 51.1%<br>- For endocarditis: mortality 24 to 28%, cross-clamp 184 to 202 min (80 to 90 min for calcific disease)<br>- Hemi-Commando (Elgharably, 37 patients): hospital death 8%<br>- ESC 2023: intervalvular fibrosa reconstruction, pooled perioperative survival 84%<small>Cardiac 8 (Sidik 2025; Perri and Johnston 2022; surgical approach; ESC 2023 section 10.3); Cardiac 4</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Mitral endocarditis: should the valve be repaired or replaced?</summary><div class="fca">- Repair when infection is limited to the leaflets (AATS I): a perforation with an intact free margin and chordae can be patched<br>- ESC 2023: attempt repair only if it will be durable and all infected tissue can be removed. Repair cannot be called superior (confounded registry)<br>- Feringa 2007: in-hospital mortality 2.3% v 14.4%, late recurrence 1.8% v 7.3%. All observational; repaired valves were less destroyed<br>- TSRA: replacement is often needed if the chordae or annulus are involved<small>Cardiac 8 (surgical approach; ESC 2023 section 10.3); TSRA ch. 67; AATS 2016</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Mechanical or biological valve in IE?</summary><div class="fca">- No difference: Flynn meta-analysis, survival HR 0.94, reinfection HR 0.95, reoperation HR 0.82 (all non-significant)<br>- Choose by the usual criteria and patient preference; avoid a mechanical valve after stroke or intracranial bleeding<br>- The Danish registry found higher recurrence with bioprostheses<br>- AHA 2026: eradicating infection matters more than the choice of substitute<small>Cardiac 8 (Sidik 2025; ESC 2023 section 11.1; AHA 2026 part 4); Cardiac 4</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>How are extracardiac foci handled around valve surgery?</summary><div class="fca">- They must be eradicated before antibiotics finish; deal with them before, during or after surgery depending on urgency<br>- Splenectomy usually before valve surgery; vaccinate afterwards<br>- Spondylodiscitis and acute renal failure do not delay urgent cardiac surgery<br>- Dental, line and device sources are dealt with or planned for<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 sections 9 and 10.1); Cardiac (timing section); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

## 13. Antibiotics after surgery

<details class="fc"><summary>When a patient is operated on mid-course, from when is the antibiotic duration counted?</summary><div class="fca">From the first day of effective therapy (the first negative culture), not the day of surgery (ESC 2023).<br>- NVE replaced by a prosthesis during treatment: continue the NVE regimen<br>- AHA 2026: staphylococcal NVE that goes to surgery stays on native-valve monotherapy (the combination regimen had more relapse and death)<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 antimicrobial part 1; AHA 2026 part 3); ESC 2023; AHA 2026</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Conflict: if the valve culture is positive, does the antibiotic course restart?</summary><div class="fca">Newest first:<br>- AHA 2026: no. Finish the planned 4 to 6 weeks, counting pre-operative days, with at least 14 days after surgery. A positive culture or PCR does not change the duration (Kim, Rao, Johansson data)<br>- [ESC 2023: start a new full course if valve cultures are positive]<br>- [Course notes: restart the full course from the day of operation; AHA 2015: full course]<br>Say which guideline you are following.<small>Cardiac 8 (AHA 2026 part 3; ESC 2023 antimicrobial part 1; surgical approach &#x27;from memory&#x27;); Cardiac (timing section); AHA 2026; ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Post-operative duration in other cases: course already complete, and NVE against PVE?</summary><div class="fca">- AHA 2026: course complete before surgery, no active infection, negative valve culture: no further antibiotics. Metastatic foci: longer, as the team decides<br>- ESC 2023: NVE 2 to 6 weeks, PVE ≥6 weeks<br>- Oral step-down: ≥10 days IV and ≥7 days after surgery, with TOE first (IIa A)<br>- Older sources: TSRA and Sidik 2025 give 6 weeks after surgery<small>Cardiac 8 (AHA 2026 part 3; ESC 2023 summary and OPAT); TSRA ch. 67; Sidik 2025</small></div></details>

## 14. Risk scores and outcomes

<details class="fc"><summary>Name the IE-specific surgical risk scores. What does PALSUSE stand for?</summary><div class="fca">AEPEI, STS IE score, PALSUSE, de Feo, ANCLA.<br>- PALSUSE: Prosthetic valve, Age ≥70, Large intracardiac destruction, Staphylococcus, Urgent surgery, Sex (female), EuroSCORE ≥10<br>- ANCLA: Anaemia, NYHA IV, Critical state, Large destruction, surgery of the thoracic Aorta<br>All are retrospective and none is in routine use. No single factor (such as age) should decide.<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 section 8.1); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>What are the operative mortality, long-term survival and recurrence after IE surgery?</summary><div class="fca">- TSRA: operative mortality 3 to 20%, highest with extension beyond the valve and with PVE; 10-year survival 50 to 60%; recurrence about 10% (5 to 12%)<br>- ESC 2023: recurrence 2 to 9%. Relapse is the same organism, usually &lt;6 months; reinfection is a new organism, usually &gt;6 months<br>- Survival after completed treatment: 85 to 90% at 1 year, 70 to 80% at 5 years<small>TSRA ch. 67; Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 section 11); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

## 15. Viva pitfalls

<details class="fc"><summary>Which three Atlas-draft errors on ESC 2023 should you avoid?</summary><div class="fca">- Persistent bacteraemia &gt;1 week is IIa B, not Class I<br>- Fungi or multiresistant organisms: urgent or non-urgent by haemodynamics (I C), not always urgent<br>- PET uptake in PVE is major whatever the time since surgery, not only after 3 months<small>Cardiac 8 (Atlas additions); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>A 12 mm mobile mitral vegetation, no embolism, valve working well. Is surgery mandatory?</summary><div class="fca">No. ESC 2023: urgent surgery may be considered (IIb B) if surgical risk is low. It becomes Class I only after an embolus despite antibiotics (I B) or with another indication (I C). EASE does not justify operating on every vegetation &gt;10 mm.<small>Cardiac 8 (ESC 2023 Rec Table 12; AATS 2016 overview, EASE caveat); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Worked case: mechanical AVR 4 years ago, S. aureus, 14 mm vegetation, rocking dehiscence, root abscess, PR 240 ms, small non-haemorrhagic infarct (NIHSS 3). What is the timing and the plan?</summary><div class="fca">- Urgent surgery (I B, locally uncontrolled infection), plus S. aureus PVE and a ≥10 mm vegetation; emergency if he becomes unstable<br>- Do not delay for the stroke: no coma and no haemorrhage, so I B<br>- Coronary CTA, not a catheter across the vegetation<br>- Radical debridement and root replacement (homograft or valved conduit); epicardial pacing if complete AV block<small>Cardiac 8 (Atlas worked case, checked against ESC 2023); ESC 2023</small></div></details>

<details class="fc"><summary>Which single brain finding warrants delaying indicated surgery by about 4 weeks?</summary><div class="fca">A significant intracranial haemorrhage (ESC 2023: &gt;1 month, IIa C), unless the patient is unstable.<br>Not a TIA, a non-haemorrhagic ischaemic stroke without coma, microbleeds or silent emboli. (AHA 2026 also delays for a major ischaemic stroke: 2 weeks, preferably 4.)<small>Cardiac 8 (Atlas SBA; ESC 2023 Rec Table 17; AHA 2026 part 4)</small></div></details>
