## Lung isolation and one-lung ventilation: additions from the FRCS Atlas HTML builds
Added 20 November 2026 after comparing the FRCS Atlas HTML builds (53-chapter merger ch. 32, section 2 and worked viva 3; Thoracic-and-Congenital "Thoracic access, lung isolation and conversion"; index_5 "Thoracic access, one-lung ventilation and analgesia"; Consolidated ch. 29) with this tab. Only points not already in the ST4 course are listed; each says whether it was checked against a primary source.

The tidal volume, PEEP, recruitment and 100% oxygen points are already in Thoracic 16 (ERAS/ESTS 2019). The points below are not.

### Physiology
- One-lung ventilation in the lateral position creates a right-to-left shunt through the collapsed operative lung. Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction diverts about 40 to 50% of that lung's blood flow to the ventilated dependent lung. (FRCS Atlas draft; not checked against a primary source)
- Anaesthetic agents and vasoconstriction. Checked: ERAS/ESTS 2019 (Batchelor et al., Eur J Cardiothorac Surg 2019;55:91-115), p. 100:
  - Older volatiles (ether, halothane) strongly inhibit hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction and caused frequent hypoxaemia.
  - Isoflurane, sevoflurane and desflurane are weak inhibitors; at 1 MAC or less, oxygenation does not differ meaningfully from total intravenous anaesthesia.
  - The Atlas says agents above 1 MAC and vasodilators blunt the response, which is consistent.
  - Volatile and intravenous techniques are equivalent choices (evidence low).

### Lung isolation
Checked: ERAS/ESTS 2019, pp. 99 to 100.
| Device | Point |
|---|---|
| Double-lumen tube | Used for most cases; more stable, so fewer repositionings and interruptions; more airway injury and sore throat |
| Bronchial blocker | Useful in a difficult airway where a large double-lumen tube is a problem |
| Either | Position with a fibreoptic bronchoscope to avoid accidental lobar obstruction |

### Protective ventilation: what the evidence does and does not show
Checked: ERAS/ESTS 2019, p. 100.
- Over three decades hypoxaemia during one-lung ventilation has become less common. The concern has moved to lung injury, both in the ventilated lung and in the collapsed lung.
- Small tidal volumes without PEEP have not clearly reduced lung injury, and larger volumes tend to cause less hypoxaemia. With PEEP, small volumes give equivalent oxygenation.
- Recruitment at the start improves oxygenation but can drop blood pressure briefly.
- CPAP to the collapsed lung reduces its local inflammatory response.
- Dexmedetomidine improves oxygenation, but has no outcome trials.
- Non-intubated thoracic surgery shows promise but is not recommended for routine use (evidence low).

### Desaturation during one-lung ventilation: the drill
This is the Atlas's model answer to a viva, SpO2 86% during right upper lobectomy. (FRCS Atlas draft; not checked against a primary source, except where stated)
1. FiO2 1.0.
2. Check the position of the double-lumen tube or blocker with a fibreoptic bronchoscope; suction secretions.
3. PEEP to the ventilated (dependent) lung.
4. CPAP about 5 cmH2O to the operative lung.
5. Tell the surgeon. If the resection is under way, clamping the pulmonary artery of the operated lung removes the shunt. The carinal resection section in Thoracic 2 uses the same manoeuvre, from a different source.
